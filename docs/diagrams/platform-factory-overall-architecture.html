Platform Factory — Overall Architecture

Git is the source of truth for everything; Kubernetes is the control plane. Teams (and later, agents) declare intent as files in repos; the repo boundary is the approval boundary — CODEOWNERS routes every change to whoever must approve it; admission policy, not human review, enforces the guardrails. The layer-0 infrastructure from the companion diagram is the floor this whole machine stands on.

three tiers of change · three approval speeds
tier 1 · fastest
Your own repo (k8s/)
Team approves its own changes — schema validation and Kyverno admission enforce the guardrails, no platform ticket.
→
tier 2 · cross-team
edge-config
Exposure to the internet is the risk — so security approves external/ via CODEOWNERS, structurally, on every PR.
→
tier 3 · slowest
systems
New tenants and quota changes reshape the platform itself — platform team approves.

a change moves to a central repo only when its approver is someone other than the team shipping it — that is the whole rule (ADR-0001)

the autonomy ladder — what M4 proves
L0 · propose
Agent opens PRs, humans do everything else
PR-only access with its own identity — every action attributable, nothing merges without a person.
→
L1 · supervised
Human approves each merge, packet in hand
The approval packet (diff, checks, blast radius, rollback) keeps review real rather than ritual.
→
L2 · earned
Auto-merge for one proven change class
Earned by accrued track record on a scorecard; an escape triggers automatic demotion back down the ladder.

The novel claim: autonomy levels enforced by branch protection + CODEOWNERS alone — promotion and demotion are config changes in the merge path, not policy documents. Nobody has published a working example; M4 builds one change class (dependency bumps) end-to-end to test it.
